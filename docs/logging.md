# The workout log

Where a day lives before and after it is saved (`state.drafts`, `state.dayDrafts`, `state.unlogged`), unticking and lining up lifts, unfinished days, and day notes. Read this whole file before changing anything it describes, and update it in the same commit when that behaviour changes (CLAUDE.md, *Docs*).

- **Three stores hold a day that is not (all) history, and they are not the same thing.** `state.drafts` is the crash/lock snapshot of whatever form is open right now. `state.unlogged` is what a SAVED day left unfilled (see the bullet below). `state.dayDrafts` is deliberate: backing out of a half-built workout parks the whole day there (`closeWorksheet()`/`stashDayDraft()`), it renders above the history in the Log tab, and nothing in it counts toward sets, PRs, volume or the charts until the day is actually committed. **There is only ever one workout for today**, which is what `actions["log-exercise"]` (the Log exercise button in the exercise detail window) has to respect: it opens the sheet you already have open, else today's parked draft `draftId` and all, else a fresh day (it never starts a second one) and then drops straight into that exercise's entry form. It is a shortcut past New Workout → Add exercise → find it in the picker, not a second way to write to the log. Inside that day it opens the lift's OWN row when the day already has one (see the next rule), and with no sheet and no parked day it opens the row saved earlier today, the way the Log tab opens one. **A parked day is written by `stashDayDraft` and read by `sheetFromParked` / `sheetForDay`, and by nothing else.** Four buttons pick one back up (*Continue* in the log, *Log this day* on the calendar, *Start* on a plan, and *Log exercise* in a lift's window), and each used to spell the same object literal out by hand. They drifted, which is the one thing four copies reliably do: the fourth never carried `planIds`, so a day started from a plan and then picked up from an exercise window came back not knowing which plan it was answering, `prunePlans` had nothing to consume on save, and the plan went on reading as *unused* on its own day forever with no way left to clear it. So **a field added to a parked day is added in those two functions, not at a call site**, the same rule `renameExerciseIn` states for everything that stores an exercise name. *Start New Workout* on Home and the ＋ button go through `sheetForDay` too: they used to open a blank sheet whatever was parked, and backing out of that parked a SECOND day on the same date that none of the other doors into today could ever find. Both COPY `planIds` rather than hand the array over, because `plan-start` pushes onto the open sheet's list and a shared array would edit the store through the back door.
- **Unticking a set is an edit, not a refusal, and a not-done row stays in the log.** `entryOnRecord` says whether the entry in the window is already a record: a card in the open day sheet (`isDraft`) *or* a row in `state.log`, which is what the Log tab opens when you tap a history row. Anything on record may be emptied and saved again (the button relabels to `entry.saveNotDone`) because unticking is someone taking back a claim about their own training and the app has no business refusing to write that down. Nothing is turned away any more: a brand-new entry with nothing in it is a lift *lined up* for later (see the bullet below). Both routes into that window must stay covered; for a long time only the sheet one was, and the Log-tab route silently greyed out at exactly the moment it was needed. The row that comes back is blank but **keeps its `plan`**, so the lift reads as planned-and-not-done, the day chip drops to 0/n, and the ghost rows are still there to tick again later. That means **`state.log` can contain entries with no numbers on them**, which is a real invariant to hold: `earlierOutings` filters them out (a not-done row is not a session you had, and leaving it in let it become the "last day you trained this" and come back empty, hiding the real one behind it), and anything rendering a row has to guard `entrySummary` with `entryHasData`. Note `commitWorkout` still drops blanks when a whole *day* is saved, and there the same lift reverts to the plan via `prunePlans` instead, which reads the same way to the user and is why both are "planned, not done".
- **A lift can be lined up with nothing in it** (`lineUp` in `entryComputed`). The entry form used to refuse a brand-new entry carrying no numbers, on the grounds that a blank row is a row nobody asked for. That was wrong about who was asking: halfway through the hamstring curls you decide the calf raises are next and you want them on the day NOW, with the numbers to follow. Being made to type 1 rep at 1 kg to get past the button and correct it afterwards turns a note into a false record. **This is not a plan**: a plan is a dated intention that can sit in the calendar for weeks and is counted by nothing, while a lined-up lift is a card in the day you are training right now and becomes an ordinary logged lift the moment you fill it in. `commitWorkout` still drops whatever is blank when the DAY is saved, so a lift you lined up and never did leaves no empty row in your history, and the sheet's footer already says so before you save.
- **A saved day keeps what you did not log, and scrapping one is a separate decision** (`state.unlogged`, `unloggedOn` / `unloggedWith` above `commitWorkout`, `actions["scrap-draft-entry"]`). Blank entries used to be dropped on save, which read one gesture as two decisions: *I skipped that lift* and *I forgot to write it down* produced the same result and only the app knew which had happened, and the one you actually did was the one you lost. So `commitWorkout` now files them under `state.unlogged` as `[{date, entries, savedAt}]`, one row per date, and `edit-day` hydrates them back into the sheet after the logged rows and the plan ghosts. **It is not the log and it is not history**: nothing in it is a set, nothing is counted toward volume, PRs, weeks or a chart, and the Log tab never draws it as a row of history (it names it as still to log, see the next bullet): the same promise `plans` and `dayDrafts` make. The other half is the bin on a waiting card: no confirm (a blank holds no numbers) and nothing is written until the day is saved, so backing out of an edit undoes it, and on a plan-linked blank it gives up the plan's claim exactly as deleting one from the sheet always has. **Plan-linked blanks are deliberately absent from it**: `prunePlans` already leaves those on the plan and `edit-day` reads them from there, so storing both would deal the same lift into the sheet twice. **Nothing waits on a lift the day already has a row for** (`stillOutstanding` / `loggedNamesOn`). *Waiting* means still outstanding, and a blank Hack Squat beside the Hack Squat you logged is not outstanding, it is the same lift twice: `edit-day` deals the logged row in regardless, so the twin can only ever arrive as a duplicate card under a lift the day says you did. Deleting the twin and backing out **without** saving used to leave it exactly where it was, which is the right rule about unsaved edits and the wrong one here, because there was nothing left for it to be waiting on. So the rule does not turn on who pressed save: `unloggedOn` drops it on the way OUT of the store as well as `commitWorkout` dropping it on the way in, which is what makes a day saved before the rule existed open clean and the next save of that day write the shorter list. Note the two ends read different sources on purpose: the read filters against `state.log`, the write against the sheet's own filled rows, which are not in the log yet. A lift the day has no row for is untouched by any of this and still comes back when an edit is abandoned. Two paths unmake a day and both must clear its row or it is orphaned where nothing can reopen it: `delete-day`, and `commitWorkout` when the save leaves the day with no filled rows at all. Being a plain `defaultState()` array it travels in a backup and defaults to `[]` on an older save with no migration, and because it files things under an exercise name *and* a group name it is in all three rename/delete sweeps (`exwin-save`, `group-save`, the group delete).
- **AN UNFINISHED WORKOUT SAYS SO** (`dayLeftovers` / `todayUnfinished` / `leftoverDays` / `leftoverNames` beside `unloggedWith`, drawn by `renderHistory`, `renderDayCard`, `renderCalendar`, `renderUnfinishedCard` and the nav). A saved day can still hold lifts with no numbers in three places: a row unticked back to nothing (`entry.notDone`), the rest of a plan the day started (`startedOn`), and the lifts it was saved without logging (`state.unlogged`). `edit-day` deals all three back in, but nothing SAID so: the history drew a day saved after its first lift exactly like a finished one. Now one helper answers "what does this day still owe" and every surface reads it: a gold *N to log* chip on the history card and on the calendar's day card, the waiting lifts listed under the day (`left.unseen`: the not-done rows are already rows, so the chip counts them and the line does not repeat them; the calendar card leaves out the rest of a plan that is drawn just below it with its own *N left*), a small gold ring in the corner of the month-grid cell (parked days included), and for TODAY a card under *Start New Workout* on Home plus a hollow ring on the Log tab. Continue is whichever door the day really has: `resume-draft` for a parked day, `edit-day` for a saved one. Today's plan card stands aside when the unfinished card is already about the rest of that plan (`plan.startedOn === today`), or Home offered the same lifts twice. **It counts lifts and is never a verdict**: a planned lift with three of four sets done is NOT in it; it has numbers, its plan line already says 3/4, and an indicator nothing could ever clear only teaches people to ignore it. Everything it counts has a way out (fill it in, or bin it), which is the test for being on it. It reads the three stores and writes none of them. The calendar's day card used to print a not-done row through `entrySummary` unguarded ("0 sets · best …"); it reads *Planned, not done* now, the rule the history always followed.
- **A day has a note of its own, and it is read back in six places** (`state.dayNotes`, `dayNoteOn` / `noteBlock` / `dayNoteBlock`, written in the *Session note* field at the foot of `renderWorkoutSheet`). An entry's `notes` is about one lift; this is about the session (how you slept, what the gym was like, why the numbers are what they are), so they are two fields answering two questions and neither is a place to write the other. It is a **map keyed by DATE**, like `goals` is keyed by a lift's name, because there is no day record to hang it on: the log is a flat list of entries carrying a date, and a day's rows come and go while the date does not. **It counts for nothing** (no PR, no volume, no week, no graph: the app never reads it) and it is **dropped with the day it belongs to**: `delete-day`, and a day emptied out and saved as not done, since a note with no session under it is a sentence about nothing. On a day MOVED to another date it goes with the day; the one thing an empty note never does is wipe a note already sitting on the destination, which the sheet was never handed and so cannot be what decides is gone: the same reasoning `unloggedWith`'s `alsoDrop` follows. It is written in **one** place and read back in six, which is the point of it: the day in the Log tab (where the note itself is a second way into `edit-day`), the day on the Calendar, a parked day above the history (where it rides on the `dayDrafts` row as `dayNote` and reaches `state.dayNotes` only when the day is committed, exactly as the entries do), the point you tap on a lift's graph, every session in a lift's history, and **`Last time` in the next workout**, which is the surface that pays for the feature: “shoulder was cold, went light” is not a fact about one lift and could never have been written on one, and it is exactly what you want in front of you stood over the bar a week later. A day holding **nothing but a note** is still parked rather than binned (`stashDayDraft`), and clearing both is what deletes a parked day; it can never be *saved* on a note alone, because a note is not a workout.
- **The history is searchable, and a search narrows the days AND the lifts in them** (`historyDaysHTML`, `ui.historyQ`, the field above the days once there is more than one; parked drafts stay above it, as they are not history yet). A day is found by its date as shown or by its day note, and then shows whole; otherwise by its lifts (the name through `nameMatches`, the muscle group, the lift's own note), and then shows only the lifts that matched, which is the answer to "how has my bench gone" without scrolling past every other lift of every day. The day's header keeps the whole day's chips, because tapping it still opens the whole day. **The history is drawn a page at a time** (`HISTORY_PAGE`, 30 days, `ui.historyShown`, *Show older* is `history-more`, patched in place): every day ever logged used to be drawn on every render of the tab, and render() runs under every tap of a workout opened from it, measured on a throttled phone at 0.1 s with a month of training, 1.3 s with a year and 3 s with two; now about 0.25 s flat. A search narrows first and pages after, leaving the tab goes back to one page, and a jump to an older day (a calendar day, a point on a graph, `ui.logJump`) opens the pages down to it.
- **One lift, one row, per day** (`mergeLift`, `sheetLift`, `dayLiftForm`, `logPut`). Every way of adding a lift used to make a new row without looking, and a preset's blank Hamstring Curl ended up beside the Hamstring Curl logged through the lift's own window, the blank (and any plan target on it) stranded. Now every door asks first: *Log exercise* and the picker open the row the day already has (its sets and its plan target with it), a preset adds only the lifts the day does not have (and *Start workout* on a preset goes into today's workout, parked day and all, rather than starting a second one over it), and a plan dealt into the day hands its target to a row of the same lift that has none. Under all of them is a net: saving a form that is not a row yet joins a row of the same lift in the sheet (`save-entry-form`), and saving the day joins rows of the same lift saved earlier that day (`logPut`, both branches of `commitWorkout`, so re-saving a day that already holds two rows of one lift makes them one). `mergeLift` is the rule: the row already there keeps its identity (id, position, plan link, superset mark); a blank row simply takes the new numbers; two rows that both hold sets become one list of sets. It refuses (and both rows stay) what it cannot join honestly: two cardio sessions, two kinds, or sets in two different units.
