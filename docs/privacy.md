# Privacy, terms, age and consent

The Privacy policy and the Terms of use, the 13+ confirmation, and the consent asked before health data (`TERMS, AGE, AND CONSENT TO HEALTH DATA` in `app.js`; `POST /v1/me/consent` and migration `0013_consent.sql` in `api/`). Read this whole file before changing anything it describes, and update it in the same commit when that behaviour changes (CLAUDE.md, *Docs*).

- **The two documents are static pages beside the app**: `privacy.html` and `terms.html`, each holding all three languages, with the one shown picked by the address's `#en`, `#sv` or `#uk` through CSS (`:target`), and each with its own strict Content Security Policy. Each language's section opens with its own language links (and a link to the app), so the jump to it never scrolls them out of view, and `legal.js`, their only script, switches language with `location.replace`, so looking at three languages does not take three back presses to leave. **The app never opens them as pages** (it used to, in a new tab: an installed app has no browser around it, so there was no way back, a back press walked back through every language looked at, and on an iPhone it opened Safari). `legalLink` is a link the app handles itself (the click dispatcher stops any link that carries an action from navigating; a button would be one unbreakable box, and the full stop after it wrapped onto a line of its own) that opens `renderLegal`, a window of the app's own with an X and the app's language switch (`legal-open`, `legal-lang`, `legal-close`): `legalLoad` reads the sections out of the same two files (in `SHELL`, so it works with no signal), switching language adds no history, every page and language opens at its top, back closes it like any window, a link from one document to the other stays in the window, and mail and IMY links open outside. It sits above everything, the Terms sheet included, and opens from the signup form, the Terms sheet, the health-consent sheet and the bottom of Settings. They state who is responsible (Brad Persson, Sweden, swagerooner@gmail.com, the same address as `VAPID_SUBJECT`), every kind of data stored with its legal basis, every other party that handles it, how long each is kept, and the rights and where in the app each is exercised. **They describe the code, so a change to what is stored, who sees it or how long it stays changes them in the same commit**, in all three languages, together with `TERMS_VERSION` (below). The inventory they were written from is the P4 data inventory in the hardening report.
- **Accepting them is recorded per account** (`users.terms_version` and `terms_at`). Signing up confirms 13 or older and accepts both on the form (`agreeBox`, action `login-agree`): Create account stays disabled until it is ticked, and `register` sends `TERMS_VERSION`, which the server stores with the time. An account that has not accepted the current version (made before the documents existed, signed up from an older build, or a new version since) gets `renderTermsGate` on its next launch or login, once `consentCheck` has asked `GET /v1/me`: a sheet with no X and no backdrop to tap, whose only ways on are accepting (`terms-accept`) or logging out; it stays up until the logout has really happened, so cancelling the "Log out?" confirm is no way past it, and a back press with nothing under it leaves the app as it does from Home, and the next launch asks again. **A new version is a new `TERMS_VERSION`** (the date it was published, which `cleanTermsVersion` on the server checks the shape of), and every account is asked again.
- **Body measurements and the sex setting are data concerning health** (GDPR article 9), so they need the account's explicit consent (`users.health_at`, `healthOk` in the app), asked before the Body Measurements window opens or the Standards' sex question is answered, on a profile of the account's own (`healthGated`; somebody else's shared profile is theirs, consented on their account). The sheet (`renderHealthGate`) says what is stored and why; *I agree* records it (`health-agree`) and carries on to what was asked for, and *Not now* stores nothing. The answer is remembered on the phone too (`zenofit:health`, per account), so a phone with no signal does not ask again, and the server's answer replaces it at each launch.
- **Withdrawing deletes the data, everywhere** (`health-withdraw`, at the bottom of the Body Measurements window, behind a confirm). The server, in one batch of set-based statements, turns every body row in every profile the account owns (deleted ones included) into a tombstone and sets `sex` back to unset, stamped like a push (`item_seq`), so every device drops them on its next pull, profiles not open on any phone included; then it clears `health_at`. The profile on screen is emptied at once, and so is the Standards form's own copy of the sex answer (`ui.std`) with the verdict worked out from it. Rows the account wrote into somebody else's profile through a write grant are that person's and stay, as with deleting an account. `smoke_sync.py` pins the server half.
- Logging out and deleting the account clear the Terms sheet and the consent from the screen, so the next account on the phone is asked for its own; deleting the account also forgets the remembered consent.
