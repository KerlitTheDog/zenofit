# Progress, charts and standards

The est. 1RM curve (`est1RM`), the line charts and their three scopes, the Body graph and weekly weigh-ins, the exercise ledger, the 1RM calculator, the strength standards (`standards.js`), and the Progress tab's three segments. Read this whole file before changing anything it describes, and update it in the same commit when that behaviour changes (CLAUDE.md, *Docs*).

The one deliberate departure from the spreadsheet is the **1RM estimate**. The sheet used Epley, which reads a single at 30 kg as a 31 kg max; the app now uses Wathan's equation divided by its own one-rep value, so one rep is exactly the weight lifted (`rmCurve`/`est1RM`, with the reasoning and the formula bake-off written up in the comment above them). Everything downstream (best set, PRs, goals, the progress graph, the calculator tab, the set suggestions) reads that one curve, so it is the only place to change it.
- **Charts** (`drawBarChart`, `monotonePath`, `niceTicks`) are hand-drawn SVG, no charting library. The progress line chart (`drawLineChart` → `paintLineChart`) is the interactive one: pinch/wheel zoom, drag to pan, tap a dot to open the entry behind it, and a fullscreen copy (`renderChartFull`). Its view (`ui.chartView`) and selection (`ui.chartSel`) live in `ui` so a re-render never resets them, in-flight pointer state is module-level (`chartPointers`/`chartGesture`) because a gesture redraws the chart underneath itself, and **every `[data-linechart]` of the same scope is painted from the same state**: the card and the fullscreen window are one chart drawn twice. Selecting a point deliberately patches `[data-linedetail]` in place rather than calling `render()`.
- **The graph does not stop at your data.** `ui.chartView[scope]` is `{lo, hi, yLo?, yHi?}` in *index* space (x) and *value* space (y), and `chartWindow`/`setChartView` deliberately allow it past both ends of the series, out to `chartMaxSpan(n)` and far enough either side that the history is nearly, but never quite, off screen. The empty space in front of the last session is the feature: a graph that ends at your best set reads as a ceiling. `yLo`/`yHi` absent means the vertical axis is still auto-fitting to whatever is on screen (`niceTicks`), which is what makes a zoomed-in plateau readable; a pinch or a vertical drag freezes it and hands it over (`ticksWithin`, which fits ticks *inside* a given domain rather than rounding the domain outward). Vertical dragging is fullscreen-only (the card in the tab has `touch-action:pan-y` because the page under it has to stay scrollable), which the paint reads off `data-chartfull` on the wrap. `chart-reset` (or a double-click) is the one way back to auto.

**There are three of that chart, told apart by scope.** `"main"` is the Progress tab's; `"ex"` is the copy at the bottom of the exercise detail window (`exWindowProgress`, fed by `exerciseHistory`), which can sit on top of the Progress tab; `"body"` is the measurement graph in the Body window (see the next bullet). The scope is the value of the `data-linechart` / `data-linedetail` / `data-charttoolbar` attribute and the `data-scope` on every chart button, and it keys the series (`chartState.line` / `.exLine` / `.bodyLine`), the zoom (`ui.chartView[scope]`), the selection (`ui.chartSel[scope]`) and which one `ui.chartFull` has blown up. Anything new that touches the chart has to carry the scope with it, or the graphs will start redrawing each other. **A graph that is not a lift says what it is** (`title` / `sub` on its series), which `renderChartFull` prefers over a lift's name and metric.
- **THE BODY, AS A LINE** (`renderBodyTrend` / `bodyChartPrep` / `bodyChartInfo` / `bodySeries` / `renderBodyPointDetail`, `BODY_METRICS`, `ui.bodyMetric`). Between the Body window's four stats and its list of check-ins, a graph of any one measurement over time: bodyweight, waist, chest, arm, thigh or glutes, picked by a chip (only measurements that were ever taken get one, and no chips at all when only one was). It IS the lift graph (the `"body"` scope), so pinch, pan, fullscreen and tap-a-dot behave exactly as they do on Progress. A dot is a CHECK-IN: its card gives the number, how far it moved since the previous check-in that measured the same thing (**in no colour**, since a smaller waist and a bigger arm are both the point and neither direction is good or bad), everything else that check-in measured, its note, and *Edit check-in* (hidden on a read-only profile). **Units are never invented**: bodyweight wears the profile's unit exactly as the stats above it do, while the tape carries none, because the form says cm/in and the app has never known which. A blank, a zero or anything that will not parse is a check-in that did not measure it, not a point at the bottom of the graph; a comma decimal from a synced row is read as a point. Fewer than two points shows the line's promise in words instead of a graph with one dot. **The series is built in `render()`, right after `chartState` is reset, not inside the Body window**, because the window is drawn AFTER the fullscreen chart in the overlay list and a fullscreen body graph needs its series first; the same pass drops a selection whose check-in is gone and folds a fullscreen body graph whose series no longer exists, so it cannot spring open the next time the window does. Switching measurement starts its zoom and its selection over. It writes nothing, so `body-metric` is on `READ_OK`. The check-in rows under it now name their measurements with the same chip labels (`bodyMetricShort`: the form's own label with its bracket taken off), where they used to print English words in every language.
- **A DAILY WEIGH-IN IS READ AS A WEEK** (`bodyWeekly`, the second row of stats in the Body window). One reading is water, salt and last night's dinner, so under the four stats sit the **7-day average** (today and the six days before; a day weighed twice counts once, as its mean) and **Per week**: this week's average minus the week before's, with that as a percentage of last week's average. That is the number a cut is steered by, and it is exactly that subtraction, nothing fitted or smoothed, so it can be checked by hand. Each week needs two weighed days or it is a reading rather than an average, so a once-a-week check-in, and somebody who stopped weighing a while ago, see nothing new (the footer says how to get it); the first week of daily weighing shows a dash and `body.perWeekWait`. It is anchored on TODAY, not on the last check-in, so old data can never pose as this week's, and a check-in dated in the future is ignored. Coloured like Change, in the profile's unit like every bodyweight here. **It is derived, never stored**: no new field, so sync, the backup and the CSV are untouched. There is deliberately no goal rate: a target has to be set by the user and travel with the profile, which is a settings change of its own.
- **Under that graph is the ledger it was drawn from** (`exerciseHistory().sessions` → `exWindowHistory`). The graph says where a lift has got to; the list says what you actually did, newest first, every set written out in the unit it was logged in. **A row is a DAY, not an entry**: two entries of the same lift on one day are one session's work and nobody remembers them as two, so they are merged, and the session's headline number and its PR flag are taken from the FIRST of its points to reach the day's best (taking the last would call a day that opened with a PR and then backed off a "match" of itself). Both readings come out of the one `exerciseHistory` call for the same reason the two graphs share a scope: a list and a graph answering different questions about one set of facts must never answer them from different facts. Rows past `EX_HISTORY_PAGE` are folded away behind one tap (`ui.exHistAll`), because somebody who has benched for two years has a hundred of them and a hundred cards would make the graph the thing you scroll past. The sets themselves come from **`outingRows`, shared with `renderLastTime`**, which is the same question ("what was that session") asked from two places.
- **Tapping a session goes to that day in the log** (`open-log-day` → `ui.logJump` → `flashLogDay`), because a date is a session you trained and the rest of what you did that day is most of what you came back for. Sending someone to a date is not the same as showing it to them, so the Log tab's history card carries `data-day` and the arriving frame scrolls to it and lights it for a moment; the marker is spent on that frame and the class goes on the node rather than into the HTML, or `render()` would replay the flash on every keystroke afterwards. A workout window in the way is closed the way its own back arrow closes it, parking the day, which is why the hint under the list says so *before* it is tapped and why the destination is set after that back-out rather than before (a plan saved on the way out lands on the calendar and would otherwise overrule the jump).
- **The 1RM calculator tab** (`renderCalc`) is stand-alone: nothing it does is logged. Its maths is `calcPct`/`calcReps` at the top of `app.js`, the same anchored Wathan curve the log runs on, read forwards and backwards, and its tables are built from the *unrounded* max so they round exactly once. The curve flattens onto an asymptote near 49.6% of the max, so `calcReps` returns `Infinity` below that and the percentage table prints `30+` rather than inventing a rep count.
- **Strength standards are a lookup, not a feed** (`renderProgStandards` and the STRENGTH STANDARDS block in `app.js`, data in `standards.js`). The Progress tab's third segment, the old PLACEHOLDER_RANKS_SLOT, answers one question about one lift: pick it, say what you weigh and what you lifted, tap. Seventy-one lifts × two sexes × nineteen bodyweights is 12,780 numbers, and **any amount of it on screen at once is a wall nobody reads**, which is why there is no ambient rank anywhere in the app and why anything new that shows one still has to go through `stdCheck()`. Nothing here is logged and nothing here writes to history, exactly like the calculator tab.
  - **The lift field takes a max OR a set, and they are the same answer** (`f.mode`, `stdMode` / `stdSetMax` / `stdLiftValue`). The tables are written in one-rep maxes and nobody trains in them, so *What you lifted* switches the field between a 1RM and reps × weight, and a set is folded into a max by `est1RM`: the log's own anchored Wathan curve, reps rounded exactly as `calc-run` rounds them. **That identity is the point**: the rank from 5 × 135 is the rank you would have got by working the max out on the 1RM tab and typing it back in, which is the trip the switch exists to save, and a second curve here would be a second answer. The running est. 1RM beside the two boxes is patched in place while typing like the hints are, and is label-plus-number only: no caption about the formula, per *The formula is a code detail, not a caption* in docs/writing.md. The two routes keep their own fields, so flipping the switch overwrites nothing and only throws the last verdict away, and `stdCheck` records the set on `res.set` so the card can name it (`std.fromSet`): a rank quoting a number nobody typed cannot be checked by hand. A rep-count standard has no max to estimate and never sees the switch.
  - **The five levels are renamed in the labels only.** The source's beginner/novice/intermediate/advanced/elite are `STD_LEVELS` = wood/gold/diamond/titanium/vibranium, drawn in `--tier-*` (per theme, in the THEMES block of `styles.css`, because a tier name has to read on a near-white card too). Nothing about a tier is persisted, so the words can change freely.
  - **It refuses to guess two things.** `settings.sex` starts `""` and the check stays disabled until it is answered, because the tables are split and a default is not a preference, it is the wrong table. It is asked in the one place it is used and is deliberately *not* in Profile. And the lift number is only ever pre-filled from the log, never for a rep-count standard on a lift logged in reps × weight: "your best set of pull-ups at bodyweight" is not in there to be read. The pre-fill is an est. 1RM, so it fills the 1RM route only; the set route starts blank, because it is for the set in front of you rather than one already on record.
  - **WHICH standard a lift of yours answers to can be said by hand** (`stdSlugFor`, `ex.std`, the *Strength standard* row in `exWindowStandard`, and the picker's `{ex}` mode). `STD_MATCH` is keyed by the shipped `default-N` ids and can never reach further than the built-ins, so a custom exercise, a variation and a built-in somebody rebuilt by hand were all invisible to the standards tab, which is most of the library for anybody who has been using the app a while. The manual answer wins and is stored **on the library row**, which is what makes it per PROFILE without a line of code saying so: a library belongs to one profile, the same movement is not even named the same in two of them, so a link made in one is meaningless in another and never travels to one. It rides along in sync and in a backup because it is part of the row, and needs no collection of its own on the wire, which also means no change to the server's `COLLECTIONS`, and an old build simply carries the field without reading it. **`""` is a real answer** and means *not this one*, so a built-in tied to a lift you do not do it as can be untied and stays untied; deleting the field instead would hand the row straight back to `STD_MATCH`. **Every lookup goes through `stdOf`**, including `stdSlugFor` on a row's own `std`: `STD_BY_SLUG` is a plain object, a row can arrive from another device, and a `std` of `"constructor"` used to name the lift's standard "Object" and stick that in the Standards form.
  - **ONE PRESS, FROM A LIFT TO A RANK** (*Check how strong you are* → `ex-std-check`, drawn by `exWindowStandard`). Getting an answer out of the standards tab used to be five steps, four of which the app could do itself while you were stood on the lift's own page: it knows the lift, it knows your best set, it knows your bodyweight and it knows your sex. So the button fills the form in and **runs the check**, lands on Progress → Standards and scrolls the rank card into view (`ui.stdJump` / `flashStdResult`, the same one-shot shape as `ui.logJump` / `flashLogDay`). The set goes in by the **set route**, never as a pre-computed max, so the card names the set it came from and the verdict can be checked by hand. `stdBestSetFor` picks THIS row's best set (not the best of everything tied to the same standard) by the highest est. 1RM, which is both the measure the graph ranks by and the number the tables are written in, so there is no second opinion to have; a rep-count standard is ranked on reps and reads only a bodyweight-kind exercise. The button is **disabled, not hidden**, with no link or no sets logged, and the line under it says which of the two is missing: a control that is not there teaches nothing. This does not make a rank ambient: it still goes through `stdCheck`, which remains the only way one is ever produced.
  - **The form remembers itself, per profile, and the bodyweight is whichever is newer** (`settings.std`, `stdForm`, `stdLatestBw`). `ui.std` **is** `settings.std` rather than a copy of it, so every keystroke is already in `state` and the next save writes it, with nowhere for the two to drift apart; it is per profile because `settings` is, and it is deliberately not one of the settings keys that sync, being the state of a question box rather than part of anybody's training. Two places in the app know what you weigh, so the rule is simply the later of the two: a value that CAME from a check-in follows the newest check-in, so logging one is enough to make every rank current, while a value TYPED here is a what-if that holds against check-ins already on record and gives way to one logged on a later day. `bwOn` is the day the figure in the box is true for, which is what makes those two cases one comparison instead of a special case each.
  - **A pre-fill hint is a claim about provenance.** "Your best from the log" / "your check-in on 12 Aug" stop being true the moment the field is typed over, and typing never causes a render, so `handleBind` retires both hints in place (`stdLiftHint` / `stdBwHint`). Bodyweight is read from the latest Body check-in and never written back; typing over it is a what-if.
  - **Thresholds are converted and rounded once, on the way out** (`stdThresholds`), so the ladder and the verdict can never disagree. Bodyweights between two 5 kg rows are interpolated; past either end of the grid the end row is used, because there is no data out there to extrapolate from.
- **1RM, Progress and Standards are three questions about your numbers behind one tab** (`renderProgress`, `ui.progSeg`). The calculator had a tab of its own in the bottom nav and did not earn one: it is a form you fill in twice a month, and it sat one row away from the two screens it belongs beside. All three are the same shape of thing (none writes to the log, all read it), so it is the Progress tab's FIRST segment now, first because it is the one that needs no history behind it. `resetTransient` still lands the tab on Progress, which is the tab's own name and what the nav icon promises, and `ui.calc` / `ui.calcResult` are still deliberately not cleared by it, exactly as the standards form is not: a question you asked should still have its answer when you come back. The segment control is handed INTO `renderCalc` rather than stacked above it, so there is one padded container and not two arguing about the gap under the pills. The slot it left in the nav is chat's.
